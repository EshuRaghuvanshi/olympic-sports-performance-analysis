# Comprehensive Video Demonstration Script
## Global Olympic Sports Performance Analysis (1992 – 2026)

**Duration:** ~8:00 Minutes  
**Audience:** Evaluators, Sports Directors, BI Specialists, NOC Strategists  
**Presenter:** Antigravity BI Lead / Analytics Director  

---

### [00:00 – 01:15] Section 1: Executive Overview & Project Setup
- **Visual:** Browser opens to `http://localhost:5000/`. Dark navy theme with Olympic rings and glowing KPI cards appears.
- **Presenter (Voiceover):**  
  *"Hello and welcome! Today we present our comprehensive Business Intelligence and Analytics platform: the Global Olympic Sports Performance Analysis covering 34 years of Olympic history from 1992 to 2026 across both Summer and Winter Games.*  
  *Analyzing Olympic success is notoriously difficult due to the volume of historical data across 60 countries and 50 sports categories. Olympic performance is not just about medals—it reveals a nation's athletic development system, sports infrastructure, and capital efficiency.*  
  *Our project delivers an end-to-end data pipeline: a relational SQLite database, fully configured native Tableau workbooks with 10 unique visualizations, 8 calculated fields, 3 responsive dashboards, a 6-scene interactive story, and this production-ready Flask analytical web platform."*

---

### [01:15 – 03:00] Section 2: Scenario 1 — Ms. Priya (NOC Strategy)
- **Visual:** Presenter clicks **"Priya (NOC Strategy)"** on the top navigation.
- **Presenter:**  
  *"Let's explore our first stakeholder scenario: Ms. Priya, a high-performance strategist at a National Olympic Committee.*  
  *Priya needs to decide which sport categories to invest in to maximize her nation's medal return per athlete fielded.*  
  *Looking at our horizontal bar chart, Priya immediately identifies the highest-yield sports: **Rowing leads the world at 6.916 medals per athlete**, followed by **Shooting at 6.731 medals per athlete**.*  
  *Cross-referencing this against our Global Performance bar chart, Priya benchmarks her country against global superpowers: the **United States with 735 total medals** and **China with 696 total medals**.*  
  *With this data, Priya recommends shifting funding into high-yield, compact disciplines like Rowing and Shooting, rather than spreading funds thinly across 20-athlete squad sports."*

---

### [03:00 – 04:45] Section 3: Scenario 2 — Mr. Carlos (Investment ROI)
- **Visual:** Presenter clicks **"Carlos (Investment ROI)"**. The bubble chart and regional pie chart load smoothly.
- **Presenter:**  
  *"Now let's look at Scenario 2: Mr. Carlos, a sports investment analyst at an international funding agency.*  
  *Carlos evaluates whether financial commitments correlate with medal achievements.*  
  *On this bubble chart of Sports Investment versus Medal Performance, Carlos spots agile overperforming nations: **Slovakia**, **Georgia**, and **Ethiopia**.*  
  *Notice how their bubble sizes indicate disproportionately high medal efficiency relative to their small investment budgets—Ethiopia, for example, achieved 36 medals with a cost per medal of just $2.08M USD.*  
  *Looking at the regional efficiency pie chart, Carlos sees that **South America leads the world at 49.64%**, followed by **Europe and Asia at 45.98%**. This directs international grant funding to agile, emerging sports hubs."*

---

### [04:45 – 06:15] Section 4: Scenario 3 — William Robert (Broadcast Storyteller)
- **Visual:** Presenter clicks **"William (Media Story)"**.
- **Presenter:**  
  *"Our third scenario features William Robert, a broadcast data journalist preparing a feature documentary series.*  
  *On our time series line chart, William shows viewers how total medals awarded swung from a **cycle low of 305 medals at Sydney 2000** to an **all-time peak of 363 medals at Tokyo 2020**, before settling at **334 medals at Milano-Cortina 2026**.*  
  *On the host nation bar chart, William highlights the dramatic home advantage: **Japan led all hosts with 105 medals in Tokyo 2020**, followed by the **United States with 100 medals in Atlanta 1996**, and **China with 93 medals in Beijing 2008**.*  
  *Our regional distribution donut chart shows how Europe and Asia have historically anchored global medal share, providing the overarching narrative for his documentary episodes."*
